# Private Design Review — reference study

This note records the real sites reviewed before building `/review/`. The goal was not to clone a page, but to identify useful structural and editorial principles for an authored, high-trust design service.

## 1. Frank Chimero — About / Studio Frank

- URL: https://frankchimero.com/about/
- Relevant because: an established individual designer speaking to early-stage teams without turning his personal site into an agency funnel.
- Observed:
  - The site behaves like an authored publication rather than a sales landing page.
  - Sparse navigation carries descriptions beside links, making the practice legible without a large pitch.
  - Monospace typography, broad empty space and an asymmetric split give the page a specific voice.
  - Credentials are stated in complete sentences and attached to real work, not separated into logo walls and metrics.
- Applied here:
  - authored-document composition;
  - asymmetric columns and margin notes;
  - direct biographical confidence without borrowed corporate polish.

## 2. Mule Design

- URL: https://www.muledesign.com/
- Relevant because: a consultancy with a strong public point of view about design and research.
- Observed:
  - Personality enters through exact wording, odd imagery and strong opinion, not decorative visual effects.
  - The consulting offer is described plainly and relatively late on the page.
  - The copy admits mistakes, stubbornness and uncertainty; authority does not require pretending to be frictionless.
  - Workshop and consulting descriptions use ordinary sentences rather than a benefits grid.
- Applied here:
  - honest boundary language;
  - first-person argument instead of generic benefit copy;
  - no testimonial carousel, client-logo strip or conversion theatre.

## 3. Julian Shapiro

- URL: https://www.julian.com/
- Relevant because: an individual researcher/creator who makes intensive authored knowledge feel like the product.
- Observed:
  - The home page treats research as a small library of substantial objects.
  - Each object has a concrete subject and one-line explanation.
  - Authority comes from visible depth ("after two million words", "each takes around 500 hours") and the body of work, rather than from generic expertise claims.
  - The page is concise and lets the work occupy the visual field.
- Applied here:
  - one concrete question is the primary object;
  - the deliverable is described as a distinct authored work;
  - research depth is explained through process rather than slogans.

## 4. Baymard Institute — Mobile App UX Audit

- URL: https://baymard.com/audits/mobile-app
- Relevant because: a real premium research-backed UX audit sold to product and UX leaders.
- Observed:
  - It is exceptionally explicit about the research base, scope, deliverables, target users, timing and confidentiality.
  - The service is made tangible through a screenshot of the actual audit output.
  - It clearly states that prototype audits can be cheaper than recoding.
  - It is also a strong negative reference for this project: sticky sales navigation, repeated calls, long testimonial sequences, checkmark lists, ROI guarantees and repeated CTAs produce a conventional enterprise funnel.
- Applied here:
  - explicit scope, terms, limits and internal sharing rights;
  - a visible description of the actual artifact;
  - no guaranteed ROI, sales call, testimonial wall or repeated conversion prompts.

## 5. Pentagram

- URL: https://www.pentagram.com/
- Relevant because: design work is presented as editorial culture rather than a conventional service catalogue.
- Observed:
  - Projects appear as authored stories with highly specific one-sentence descriptions.
  - The site is willing to interrupt regular structure with essays, quotations and thematic groupings.
  - Large typographic statements and imagery do not need card containers.
- Applied here:
  - sectional rhythm based on editorial spreads rather than feature cards;
  - one strong visual interlude;
  - large statements supported by specific explanatory copy.

## 6. iA

- URL: https://ia.net/
- Relevant because: a design-led company with a long-standing public critique of generic web and product conventions.
- Observed:
  - Typography carries most of the information hierarchy.
  - Product language remains short and concrete.
  - The editorial archive and the products coexist, showing that point of view is part of the business.
- Applied here:
  - type and rules do the work before components and decoration;
  - short labels, concrete nouns and restrained colour.

## Synthesis used for `/review/`

The page is a **Decide / Learn** surface, but not a conventional landing-page funnel. Its composition is a private working document or research dossier:

1. the offer and terms appear immediately;
2. the page tests the quality of the question before describing benefits;
3. method is presented as an argument against context-free best practices;
4. the deliverable is made concrete;
5. epistemic and operational limits receive as much space as the promise;
6. the only action is to email one question.

Deliberately omitted:

- testimonials that do not yet exist for this service;
- borrowed client logos;
- benefit cards and icon grids;
- multiple CTA bands;
- artificial scarcity;
- calls, workshops and custom proposals;
- claims that the review will increase revenue or prevent an error.
