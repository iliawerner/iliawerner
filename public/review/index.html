One question, researched properly.

If your team is about to make an important interface decision, I will investigate it independently and make a private film with my conclusion, the evidence behind it, and what still needs testing.

I take on one project a month. The fee is US $5,000.

Send me the question

Start with the decision.

Send me the decision your team is preparing to make, the alternatives you are considering, and the materials that explain the product and its constraints.

For example
We are replacing a familiar professional workflow. Which parts carry value that may not be obvious from the interface alone, and what should the new model preserve?

The question may concern navigation, interaction models, AI and user control, a mature workflow, or the character of a product being redesigned.

How I approach the question.

Best for whom? In which product? At what point in history?
01

I read your materials.

The current product, the proposed change, its constraints, and the arguments already made inside the team. If the question is too broad, I ask you to narrow it in writing.

02

I look outside the product.

I search for historical precedents, related interaction models, research, failed attempts and useful contradictions. An analogy earns its place only if the mechanism is relevant, not because two screens look alike.

03

I make an argument.

The result is my independent view: what I think the team should preserve, change, test first, or abandon. Facts, interpretation and uncertainty remain visibly separate.

What you receive.

The film is the main work. It comes with a searchable text version and a short written summary, making the argument easy to navigate, quote and share.

01

The conclusion, the important risks, and the next action in a form that can enter a memo or product document.

Source notes

The research and historical examples behind the argument, with links for the team to continue reading.

When another kind of research would help more.

This format works best when a decision can be examined through design analysis, historical precedents and desk research. It cannot observe users I have not met or data I have not seen.

I may not be the best choice when the main task is:

  • user interviews or usability testing
  • product analytics or conversion analysis
  • technical, legal or accessibility compliance work
  • a complete redesign or an implementation specification
