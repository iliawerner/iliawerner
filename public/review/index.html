The brief

A useful question has consequences.

I am not offering a general audit of your product. The review starts with one decision your team has not made yet, and one that will be expensive to reverse after development begins.

What do you think of our app?
We are replacing a familiar professional workflow. What are we in danger of removing before we understand why it exists?

The question may concern navigation, interaction models, AI and user control, a mature workflow, or the character of a product being redesigned. It should not secretly contain five different projects.

How I work

I do not start with best practices.

Best for whom? In which product? At what point in history?
01

I read your materials.

The current product, the proposed change, its constraints, and the arguments already made inside the team. If the question is too broad, I ask you to narrow it in writing.

02

I look outside the product.

I search for historical precedents, related interaction models, research, failed attempts and useful contradictions. An analogy earns its place only if the mechanism is relevant, not because two screens look alike.

03

I make an argument.

The result is my independent view: what I think the team should preserve, change, test first, or abandon. Facts, interpretation and uncertainty remain visibly separate.

What comes back

Something your team can use without me in the room.

The film is the main work. The written material exists so nobody has to search through forty minutes of video for the conclusion.

01

A private research film

Usually 30–45 minutes, with chapters and the evidence shown on screen.

02

A short decision note

The conclusion, the important risks, and the next action in a form that can enter a memo or product document.

03

The source map

Research, historical examples, what each one contributes, and where the analogy stops being useful.

04

What remains unknown

The claims that still require your analytics, user research, technical work or a small experiment.

A necessary boundary

I will not pretend to know what the evidence cannot tell me.

This is design analysis and desk research. It can expose assumptions and improve a decision. It cannot observe users I have not met or data I have not seen.

I will decline the project if the question is better answered by:

  • user interviews or usability testing
  • product analytics or conversion analysis
  • technical, legal or accessibility compliance work
  • a complete redesign or an implementation specification
  • an outside voice hired only to validate a decision already made
Working terms · first edition

Kept deliberately small.

Scope

Fee
US $5,000. Each review is a separate project, not a subscription.
Timing
Usually ten working days after I accept the question and receive the materials.
Communication
Written and asynchronous. There is no sales call, discovery call, workshop or team management.
Acceptance
I read the brief before payment. I may ask you to narrow it, refer you to another kind of research, or decline it.
Confidentiality
The film is private and may be shared inside your company. I do not publish the client, question or findings without explicit permission.
Corrections
I correct factual mistakes. A new question or a changed brief is a new review.
Start with the question
