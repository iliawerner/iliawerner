The decision

This is useful before something becomes costly to reverse.

The review is for a team that already has a real product, real constraints and a decision with consequences. It is not a general score for the interface. It answers one question clearly enough to change what the team does next.

01

You are replacing a familiar professional workflow and need to know what the new model must preserve.

02

You are adding AI or automation and need to decide where the user should keep control.

03

You are simplifying a mature product and need to distinguish accidental complexity from expertise encoded in the interface.

The shape of the work

Evidence becomes a recommendation.

Best for whom? In which product? At what point in history?
Question
Which parts of this established workflow should survive when we replace it?
Evidence sought
The history of the workflow, failed attempts to simplify it, adjacent interaction models, relevant research, product constraints and the arguments already made inside the team.
Interpretation
Which friction is accidental, which friction carries control or expertise, and where an apparently cleaner model moves cost somewhere less visible.
Verdict
What to preserve, what to change, what to test first and what I believe the team should abandon.

This illustrates the structure of a review, not a claim about a past client result.

Before purchase

Have the context ready.

The review starts after payment and a complete written intake. You should be able to provide all of this without a meeting:

  • The decision in one sentence
  • The alternatives being considered
  • The date the decision must be made
  • The product context and constraints
  • The materials your team is ready to share: screens, prototypes, research, internal arguments or relevant data

This format is based on design analysis and desk research. User interviews, usability testing, analytics work, legal review, accessibility compliance and implementation require a different kind of engagement.
