01 / Brief

What should a mature interface preserve while the system around it changes?

Decision

Whether to remove a long-lived visual cue for systemic consistency or preserve it as part of product continuity.

Available material

The public film’s historical and comparative record: Aqua, early criticism, Windows Luna and Metro, twenty-five years of redesign, and the move to iPadOS.

Alternative A

Neutralise or remove the distinctive coloured controls to make the interface more formally consistent.

Alternative B

Retain the identity-bearing cue while simplifying mechanics that no longer serve the product.

02 / Evidence map

Separate what the record shows from what the author infers.

E–01Observed in public record

The investigation traces the coloured controls from Aqua through repeated redesigns and changing interface languages.

E–02Observed in public record

Competing systems—including Luna and Metro—made different choices while Apple repeatedly protected the cue.

I–01Interpretation

Long continuity can indicate that a visually minor detail carries recognition and product identity beyond its original mechanics.

U–01Unknown

Persistence alone does not prove user value. This specimen contains no behavioural study, client constraints, implementation cost, or observed outcome.

03 / Alternatives under stress

The choice is not old versus new. It is coherence versus continuity.

Alternative A · reject

Remove the cue for systemic consistency.

StrengthCleaner formal system and less inherited decoration.
RiskErases a recognition cue without evidence that the cue is harmful.
ReversibilityTechnically reversible; identity loss may be harder to diagnose.

Alternative B · support

Preserve the cue; modernise obsolete mechanics.

StrengthMaintains continuity while allowing surrounding interaction to evolve.
RiskCan mistake brand inertia for genuine user value.
ReversibilitySupports gradual simplification and a direct comparison test.

04 / Supported position

Preserve what carries recognition. Test what only looks familiar.

The available historical evidence supports retaining the identity-bearing cue while modernising mechanics around it. It does not establish that every inherited detail is valuable.

ConfidenceMedium
Strongest objectionTwenty-five years of persistence may reflect organisational preference or inertia rather than user value.
Next testCompare preserved and neutralised variants with the target users before a high-cost change.
Outcome evidenceNone. This is a public specimen, not a client result.
